Kettering University: Implementing a University Website

Project overview
Kettering University undertook a comprehensive redesign and rebuild of its public‑facing website to align the institution's digital presence with its modern academic priorities and co‑op‑based learning model. Carnegie Higher Ed served as the design and strategy partner, developing the site's information architecture, user‑experience patterns and visual direction. Knectar executed the implementation—upgrading the Drupal CMS, building custom templates and components, migrating content, integrating external data sources and ensuring accessibility compliance.

Goals

  • Modernize the platform – Upgrade Kettering University's Drupal installation to Drupal 10 and replace legacy templates and paragraphs with a component‑based system.
  • Reflect the university's priorities – Build templates that showcase academic programs, experiential co‑op education and research while supporting the institution's shift toward advanced mobility, new energy vehicles, intelligent manufacturing, artificial intelligence and sustainability.
  • Improve usability and accessibility – Consolidate content types, adopt a consistent design language and implement accessible markup so that visitors (including those with disabilities) can easily navigate and consume content.
  • Integrate data sources – Connect the website to external systems for program data, events, news, social media and faculty profiles.
  • Enable phased roll‑out – Adopt an incremental implementation strategy that allows the university to review each release, conduct quality assurance and train content owners before the next phase begins.

Implementation strategy
Carnegie Higher Ed provided a detailed implementation strategy that guided the project timeline. The strategy broke the work into four phases, beginning with pre‑phase activities and culminating in optional enhancements if budget allowed.

Pre‑phase and Phase 1 – foundation

  • Component inventory and planning – Before development began, Kettering reviewed and approved the inventory of components to be built. Unused paragraphs from the legacy site were pared down to reduce bloat.
  • Global theme updates – The first phase focused on updating the global theme and style system based on the Carnegie design. This baseline allowed new components to inherit the updated visual language.
  • Legacy template review – The team performed quality assurance on legacy templates to ensure that the new theme did not introduce regression issues. Content managers were involved to validate that theming changes preserved content integrity.
  • Stakeholder socialization – Kettering collaborated with Carnegie to plan the internal roll‑out of the new information architecture and global styles.

Phase 2 – new templates and program finder
Phase 2 ran from March to mid‑June. Key deliverables included:

  • Program detail template – A new content type ("Program Detail") was fielded to support program pages and the program finder. Each program includes dedicated fields (e.g., program name, college, department, contact email and phone) and a "Contact Admissions" block that can vary by program. The schema is based on the tertiary layout.
  • Homepage and program finder – The new homepage and associated paragraphs were created. The program finder is a stand‑alone view that provides a filterable list of programs. This view was integrated into the homepage and program pages.
  • Academic program page – New paragraphs were built for program landing pages, including hero sections, stats panels, feature panels and calls‑to‑action.
  • No content freeze – Content editing could continue on the existing site during development; final reconciliation occurred at the end of the phase.

Phase 3 – audience gateways and admissions
Phase 3 ran from June to July. Deliverables included:

  • Audience gateway ("Student One‑Stop") – A large umbrella page designed to consolidate key resources for students; this required a content freeze or syncing process due to the breadth of content.
  • Admissions & Aid page – A template for admissions content, aligning design patterns with other major pages.
  • Menu structure alignment – Developers provided an environment for stakeholders to review and socialize the new navigation/IA structure.
  • Campus socialization – Kettering managed stakeholder communication and migration planning.

Phase 4 – optional enhancements
The final phase (July–August) included optional work if budget allowed:

  • Additional templates – A card view for the program finder and templates for the admissions blog landing and post pages.
  • Course catalog integration – Carnegie estimated 20–30 hours to build an integration with the course catalog (not including design work).
  • Content reconciliation – A slower content editing pace followed by a final reconciliation at the end of the phase.

Technical implementation by Knectar
While Carnegie provided design and strategy, Knectar executed the technical implementation. The work spanned a large Drupal upgrade, custom theming, paragraph and view development, integrations and accessibility compliance.

Drupal 10 upgrade and content architecture
Kettering's legacy site used a collection of templates and paragraphs that had accumulated over time. Knectar upgraded the site to Drupal 10 and built a component‑based system. Templates correspond to common page types—Home, Secondary, Tertiary, Tertiary‑Minimal, Admissions, Co‑Op, Program, News Detail, Event Detail, Faculty Detail, Program Finder, Faculty Directory and Department Landing. For each template, corresponding paragraph components were created. The project documentation lists global components such as Hero, Stats Panel, Latest News, Featured Events, Instagram Feed, CTA Panel, Intro Text block and various multi‑column panels. These reusable components enable content editors to build diverse layouts while maintaining consistency.

Program and course integration
The Program Detail content type provides a structured schema for academic programs. It includes fields for the program name, associated college and department, contact information and a program‑specific admissions call‑to‑action. Knectar mapped data from existing program pages into this schema and implemented the Program Finder view to display programs in a filterable list.

News and events
Knectar created News Detail and Event Detail content types to support list views. The news schema includes fields for title, date, tags, image, teaser and body. The event schema includes fields such as title, subtitle, taxonomy tag, date, time range, ticket information, location, registration link, body, image and caption. These fields allow content editors to manage events and news articles while automatically generating lists and featured panels.

Faculty schema import and directory
One of the critical integrations was the Faculty Detail schema. The documentation notes that this content type is "fielded specifically to support Faculty List views and/or the Directory" and is based on the tertiary layout. Knectar implemented a Faculty Directory view that displays faculty in a filterable list.

Because faculty profiles already existed in external systems, Knectar built an import routine to map faculty data—name, title, department, contact information and biography—into the new Faculty Detail content type. The mapping ensured that the site could automatically update faculty profiles when data changed in the source system. The directory view uses exposed filters (e.g., by department or keyword) and links each listing to a full profile page, maintaining search‑engine‑friendly URLs and rich schema markup.

Social media and third‑party integrations

  • Instagram feed – The global "Instagram Feed" component embeds a photo carousel labeled "Connect with #ketteringuniversity," using custom HTML with an API link and Juicer integration.
  • CTA panels and visual router – Several components provide calls‑to‑action and image grids, empowering editors to highlight priority content.
  • Forms and Slate – The project includes templates for form pages that integrate with Kettering's Slate system to handle requests for information and other inquiry forms.
  • Future course catalog integration – As an optional enhancement, the team considered integrating the course catalog to surface course data directly on program pages.

Accessibility and quality assurance
Knectar ensured that all new components conformed to WCAG guidelines. Templates use semantic HTML, ARIA attributes and proper heading hierarchies. Components such as heroes, stats panels and visual router grids were designed to be navigable by screen readers and accessible via keyboard. Knectar also implemented alt‑text fields on images and provided editors with guidance on writing meaningful descriptions. QA rounds in each phase included cross‑browser testing and accessibility checks to verify compliance.

Outcomes and impact

  • Modernized, flexible site – The Drupal 10 upgrade and component‑based architecture provide Kettering with a modern, maintainable platform. Editors can assemble pages from reusable paragraphs while maintaining brand consistency.
  • Enhanced user experience – The new site highlights Kettering's academic strengths and unique co‑op program through well‑structured templates and dynamic lists (e.g., program finder, news and events, faculty directory). Clear navigation and consistent design improve the visitor journey.
  • Integrated data and automation – The implementation connects external data sources—program data, faculty profiles, social media and Slate forms—reducing manual data entry and ensuring up‑to‑date information. The faculty schema import and program finder are examples of this automation.
  • Accessibility compliance – By prioritizing accessible markup and testing, the new site provides an inclusive experience for users with disabilities and positions Kettering favorably for ADA compliance.
  • Collaborative phased approach – The four‑phase strategy allowed Kettering to review each release, perform quality assurance and manage content reconciliation without disrupting operations. The collaborative process ensured that the design vision provided by Carnegie Higher Ed was faithfully implemented by Knectar.

Conclusion
Kettering University's website project demonstrates how a collaborative partnership between a design/strategy firm and a technical implementation team can produce a modern, accessible and data‑driven digital experience. Carnegie Higher Ed's research and design insights shaped the site's information architecture and visual language, while Knectar's technical expertise brought the vision to life through a Drupal 10 upgrade, component‑based templates, custom integrations and rigorous accessibility testing. The result is a scalable, user‑friendly website that reflects Kettering's innovative spirit and supports prospective students, current students, faculty and alumni alike.
