Project context and goals
Temple University's Lewis Katz School of Medicine (LKSOM) wanted to modernize its public‑facing website to better communicate its academic programs, research achievements and community outreach. The existing site offered limited flexibility for content administrators, lacked a unified visual identity and did not meet accessibility standards. Temple engaged Transit Authority Figures (TAF) – designers Rob and Damia – to create a bold design and information‑architecture strategy, and partnered with Knectar to build the site on Drupal. The project delivered:

  • A new homepage built around a dynamic slideshow and clear calls‑to‑action. Specifications called for a slideshow that could become static when only one slide was enabled, with fields for an image, headline, caption, caption positioning and destination URL. Administrators also needed a Quick Links field and a block‑placer so they could assemble homepage components.
  • Flexible inner pages that allowed content editors to assemble body copy, slideshows and related story blocks. NodeEmbed was used so editors could embed slideshows within page bodies without writing code.
  • A searchable faculty directory. Users can search by name, browse alphabetically or filter by research centre, basic science department or clinical department. Predictive search suggests names as the user types and clicking a suggestion takes them directly to the person's profile.
  • A modular content‑management system using Workbench modules. Roles – Root, Developer, Site Admin, Content Admin and Content Editor – control permissions; content editors can create or edit pages but cannot publish them without approval.

From the outset, accessibility was a core requirement. Temple University's Web Accessibility Standards specified that all fonts use relative sizes and that text must be resizable up to 200 percent without loss of content. The site also needed alt‑text fields for every image and compliance testing using Temple's internal tools or public ADA scanners. Knectar was responsible for meeting these standards during implementation.

Partnership and roles
The project was a three‑way collaboration:

  • Transit Authority Figures (Rob and Damia) – Led design and user‑experience strategy. Their research and design work resulted in a vibrant, image‑rich layout that highlights the school's mission and programs. The homepage design uses large visuals and typography to draw attention to strategic messaging.
  • Temple University web team – Provided content, approved designs and assisted with institutional integrations such as Google Custom Search and the university events feed.
  • Knectar – Implemented the site on Drupal, built custom modules, implemented accessibility features, integrated third‑party systems and performed quality assurance. Knectar also coordinated with Temple IT on hosting and deployment.

Implementation highlights

Translating design into a responsive Drupal theme
Knectar built a responsive Drupal theme based on TAF's designs. The homepage slideshow models the smooth left‑to‑right transition used by Stanford Medicine and includes pagination dots, headlines and captions. When only one slide is published, the slideshow becomes a static hero image, allowing the homepage to retain its impact without unnecessary animation. A Quick Links module provides persistent links to high‑traffic destinations, and a block placer lets administrators assemble rows of news, events and posts. Knectar used the Paragraphs module for complex layouts, giving editors control over text, images and calls‑to‑action.

Flexible content types and workflows
The site defines custom content types for news, events, blogs, faculty profiles and static pages. Each content type has fields for titles, summaries, body content and media. News nodes include a "push to" option that determines whether the story appears on the homepage or related‑stories blocks. A scheduler module allows editors to set future publish dates. The Workbench suite provides drafting and moderation workflows so that editors can save content and submit it for publication without having direct publishing rights. Revision capturing and the Diff module enable version comparisons and rollback.

Faculty search and directories
Knectar implemented a sophisticated faculty directory. The list page includes breadcrumbs, print and text‑size controls and ShareThis buttons. Users can search by name, browse alphabetically or filter by research centre, basic science or clinical department. Predictive search suggests names as users type and takes them directly to the corresponding profile when selected. Detail pages include a profile photo, contact information, tabbed content ("About me," "Education, Training & Credentials," "Publications") and related content blocks. Temple's editors can assemble faculty lists using Paragraphs, mixing people entries and rich‑text segments in any order.

Accessibility compliance
To meet Temple's accessibility guidelines, Knectar implemented several measures:

  • Relative font sizes and text resizing – All typography is specified in relative units so that visitors can enlarge text up to 200 % without losing content or functionality.
  • Alt‑text for images – Image fields include an enabled alt‑text field so editors are prompted to describe every image.
  • Color and contrast – The design's red and white palette was tested to ensure sufficient contrast for users with low vision. Call‑to‑action buttons have clear focus states and the site works with keyboard navigation.
  • Accessible navigation and forms – Menus and form fields are built semantically using Drupal's markup and ARIA attributes. A print‑friendly view strips extraneous styling and provides a simplified layout.

Knectar coordinated with Temple's accessibility office to run automated and manual scans, addressing issues highlighted by tools like Cynthia Says.

Third‑party integrations
Knectar integrated several external services:

  • Events feed – The site pulls an XML feed from Temple's events calendar (events.temple.edu) to populate an events block. Department pages automatically filter events based on matching tags.
  • Google Analytics and ShareThis – Standard Drupal modules integrate analytics tracking and social‑sharing buttons. ShareThis appears on inner pages, news and blog posts, enabling visitors to share content easily.
  • jReject and Chosen – jReject notifies users of unsupported browsers, while the Chosen JavaScript plugin enhances select menus with search and improved accessibility.
  • Google Custom Search – Site search results are powered by Google Custom Search and styled to match the site's look and feel.

Outcome and impact
The new LKSOM website launched with a modern look and improved user experience. The homepage features a prominent hero message, "Do More Good," set over photos of students and faculty medicine.temple.edu. Below the hero area, visitors encounter the school's vision statement—"We will inspire a new standard of excellence in education and research and lead social change in medicine"—paired with quick links to programs such as the MD Program, Physician Assistant Program, Graduate Medical Education and the Health Justice and Bioethics Program medicine.temple.edu. News items and community spotlights call attention to research awards and outreach activities; for example, a featured story notes that Katz welcomed local high‑school students for a hands‑on Health Careers Exploration Day medicine.temple.edu.

The new site improves content management for Temple's communications team. Editors can create pages, news, events and faculty profiles using standardized forms and easily rearrange components through the block‑placer. The predictive faculty search and filter system make it simple for prospective students and researchers to find faculty by name or department. Integrations with the events feed and social‑sharing tools ensure that content remains fresh and widely disseminated.

Finally, the project demonstrates the value of collaboration between design, development and institutional stakeholders. TAF's strategic design vision, combined with Knectar's implementation expertise and Temple's content leadership, produced a site that communicates the school's mission, fosters engagement and meets rigorous accessibility and technical standards.
