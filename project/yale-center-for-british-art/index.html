Yale Center for British Art: Website and Collections

  • Role: Technical Lead, Developer
  • Budget: $50,000 - $100,000
  • Technology: Drupal
  • Design Partner: Fabrique
  • URL: https://britishart.yale.edu/

Background and project goals
The Yale Center for British Art (YCBA) is a leading art museum whose digital presence must support exhibitions, programs, scholarly research and access to a collection of more than 2,000 paintings, 250 sculptures, ~20,000 drawings and watercolors, 40,000 prints, 35,000 rare books and manuscripts, and an additional reference library of 40,000 volumes.

Prior to 2019 the YCBA operated a large Drupal‑7 site for the museum and a separate BlackLight‑based Ruby on Rails application for its collections search. Knectar and design partner Fabrique were engaged to design and implement a modern digital platform that would unify the visitor experience while meeting strict accessibility and technical requirements. Key goals included:

  • Modern platform & UX. Replace the aging web properties with Drupal 10/11 websites on Pantheon that match Fabrique's modern designs and provide an intuitive experience across devices.
  • Search & discoverability. Provide robust search across exhibitions, events and the collection. The museum site uses Drupal's built‑in search with result weighting for keywords, sticky/promoted content and recency. The collections site implements a headless search front‑end backed by Apache Solr, giving visitors facets such as collection, creator, date range, on‑site access, image use and classification.
  • Accessibility & compliance. Comply with WCAG 2.0 Level AA, following Yale's web‑accessibility checklist and implementing editorial workflows for content review.
  • Scalability & maintainability. Host on Pantheon's managed infrastructure for stability and ease of deployment, utilise reusable modules and contribute Solr integration code back to the Drupal community.

Main museum website (Drupal 10 on Pantheon)

Content architecture & navigation
Fabrique's design emphasised clear navigation with dedicated channels for Exhibitions & programs, Collections, Research & learning, Plan your visit and About us. The Drupal build implements:

  • Custom content types & taxonomies. The technical specification defines multiple content types (Events, Exhibitions, Generic content, People, Story) and taxonomies such as Event Type, Tag, Page Type and People Type. These allow site editors to structure and filter content intuitively.
  • Dynamic navigation. Menu items can display thumbnails, event types, titles, dates and teaser text. For example, the "What's on" menu automatically lists the two most recent exhibition nodes unless overridden by a "Sticky at top of lists" flag.
  • Flexible page templates. Templates cover the homepage, event and exhibition overviews, past events, stories, press listings, people profiles and search results. Each includes fielded content (title, hero image, featured sections, video blocks, etc.), enabling editors to populate layouts without developer assistance.

Search & SEO
Drupal search with weighting. At launch the site uses Drupal's standard search, with weightings for keyword relevance, sticky/promoted content and recency. Results pages show the query, number of results, filter options (relevance or newest first) and a link to the collections search.

SEO enhancements. Knectar implemented the Real‑time SEO for Drupal module (Yoast replacement) and Simple Sitemap to automatically generate XML sitemaps and handle meta tags. Google Analytics integration and meta‑data support allow better marketing insight.

Third‑party integrations & features

  • Bedework feed for events. Event data is exported to a feed consumed by Yale's Bedework calendar.
  • Google Tag Manager & analytics. GTM code is embedded for analytics and marketing.
  • YouTube video support. Video blocks embed videos hosted on YouTube.
  • Google Places API to display real‑time opening hours on the homepage.
  • CAS (Central Authentication Service) for single sign‑on (SSO) for internal editors.
  • Mandrill for transactional email handling.

Editorial workflow & user roles
The site defines user roles (Super‑admin, Admin, Editor, Author) and uses the Workflows module to support a structured editorial process: save draft, content review, approval, publish and unpublish. This process ensures content quality and accessibility compliance before publication.

Accessibility & compliance
YCBA required compliance with WCAG 2.0 Level AA; the technical specification references Yale's accessibility checklist. Knectar implemented accessible navigation, semantic HTML and ARIA roles, ensured proper contrast ratios, and tested with screen readers. A similar process applies to the new collections site (see below).

Infrastructure
Pantheon hosting. The site is hosted on Pantheon under Yale's account with SSL enabled by default. Knectar configured Dev/Test/Live environments and Git‑based deployment workflows.

Modules & tooling. Additional modules include Redirect (bulk redirect management), Focal Point (image handling), Mandrill (email), XML Sitemap and Metatags.

Collections search (headless Drupal 11 on Pantheon)

Motivation and data sources
The existing BlackLight Ruby on Rails application served as the collections search, indexing object metadata, artist metadata and library holdings in a Solr schema. The aim of the new project is to bring this functionality into a modern, headless Drupal environment while preserving the features that researchers rely on. The collections hold more than 56,000 prints and drawings, 39,000 library items, 21,000 rare books and manuscripts and thousands of other works.

Architecture
Headless Drupal 11. The collections search is implemented as a decoupled front‑end (Nuxt/Next‑style) that communicates with Drupal via APIs. Drupal acts as an API and configuration backend while Solr remains the authoritative search index.

Remote Solr integration module. Knectar built a custom module to query an authenticated Solr instance. The specification describes using dynamic fields (e.g., *_ss, *_txt, *_i) and copyfields for date and geo facets. Query configuration (facets, fields, boost queries) mirrors the former BlackLight setup. Generalizing this module as a contributed project delivers reusability, extensibility and community collaboration.

Faceted search UI. Visitors can filter by collection, creator, date range, on‑site access, image use, image availability, classification and other fields. Results support pagination, sorting (e.g., by date or title), multiple view modes (tile/masonry or list) and an accessible IIIF viewer for object images.

Headless artist pages & new content types. Phase Ib introduces artist facets on landing and results pages and dedicated artist pages with metadata similar to object records. Future phases will support story/essay pages with IIIF images and align the collections landing page with the main museum homepage.

Migration phases
The collections project is staged:

  • Phase I (through March 2025). Technical discovery, analysis of legacy code and BlackLight search logic; creation of Pantheon environments; development of new custom Drupal theme; implementation of remote Solr API integration and query modules; basic accessibility and performance testing. After content review and ADA compliance testing, a soft launch will publish the collections data via Drupal while retaining the existing design.
  • Phase Ib. Full transition of data and functionality to Drupal 10/11. Deliverables include implementing the high‑level architecture (object, artist and library metadata), replicating all BlackLight views (landing page, search results, object records) and adding new artist facets and pages. URL parameters such as search field, pagination, sorting and view modes must be preserved to maintain deep linking.
  • Phase II (design update). From January 2025, Fabrique will provide redesigned templates. Knectar will update the Drupal themes, refine query logic for artist lists, and support story content. The redesigned site is expected to publish by June 2025.

Compliance and testing
Each phase includes UI testing across supported browsers, accessibility testing (WCAG Levels 1 and 2), technical infrastructure compliance, performance and load testing, disaster recovery and operational compliance. The site will undergo review by Yale's ITS Accessibility team before launch.

Impact and results

  • Unified digital experience. Visitors now experience a coherent design language across the main museum site and collections search, with intuitive navigation and clear calls to search the collection.
  • Enhanced discoverability. The new sites provide multiple entry points for exploration, from exhibitions and events on the museum site to powerful faceted search across the collection (including 56k+ works on paper, 39k library items and 21k rare books).
  • Accessibility leadership. Implementing WCAG 2.0 Level AA compliance ensures the site is usable by people with disabilities. The case study emphasises physical and digital accessibility, including accessible navigation, contrast ratios and screen‑reader testing.
  • Performance & maintainability. Hosting on Pantheon and using modern Drupal ensures scalable performance, streamlined deployments and security. The remote Solr integration decouples the search backend for flexibility and caching.
  • Community contribution. Knectar's Solr module is designed for generalisation and contribution back to the Drupal community, enabling other institutions to leverage remote Solr search.

Conclusion
The Yale Center for British Art's digital transformation demonstrates how a heritage institution can modernise its web presence while honouring accessibility and scholarly requirements. By partnering with Fabrique for design and Knectar for technical execution, the YCBA successfully launched a Drupal 10 museum site and is nearing completion of a headless Drupal 11 collections search backed by Apache Solr. The project showcases best practices in modular content architecture, faceted search, accessibility compliance and open‑source contribution, positioning the YCBA for future growth and engagement.
